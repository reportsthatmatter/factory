Drafted 2026-11-07 to `.claude/skills/post-writing/SKILL.md`. Lengths are hook characters (ASCII plus straight quotes, so characters = graphemes). Post total = hook + 2 + source line ("— Mueller Report, p. N" 22–24; "— Leveson Report, p. N" 24–25; "— 2017 Intelligence Community Assessment, p. N" 46); all recommended posts are at or under 283. Paragraph ids are from `assets/generated/reports/<id>/full-body.html`.

Avoided angles from the queued hooks: Mueller DNC-hack/June 2016 release (25 Oct), the OLC opinion (27 Oct), McGahn and the 17 June call (1 Nov), IRA/St Petersburg (31 Oct); ICA 6 January publication (28 Oct); Leveson "recognised in law" and "what that law was not" (26 Oct, 6 Nov), PCC "join or leave"/funded by industry (4 Nov), Bristol landlord (29 Nov), Guardian voicemail story (2 Dec).

### 2026-12-09 uk-leveson-inquiry:1-1-press-complaints-commission:c827f121

Card: "The Press Complaints Commission (PCC) has always been a voluntary system based on a network of implied contracts." (p. 1515, Leveson's own words, Part J Chapter 4 opening)

Candidates:
- A: Four chairs of the Press Complaints Commission gave evidence to the Leveson inquiry. Lord Justice Leveson chose to focus on systems, not blame individuals. His chapter on the commission begins with how it was built.
- B: Lord Black of Brentwood was director of the Press Complaints Commission from 1996 to 2003, and later chaired the company that collected the levy funding it. Lord Justice Leveson's chapter on the commission opens with what held it together.
- C: Newspapers and magazines in the Press Complaints Commission paid a levy, collected by a separate company, the Press Board of Finance. Lord Justice Leveson's chapter on the commission opens with what bound them to it.

Recommended: Four chairs of the Press Complaints Commission gave evidence to the Leveson inquiry. Lord Justice Leveson chose to focus on systems, not blame individuals. His chapter on the commission begins with how it was built.

Length: 215 (post 242)

Sources:
- Four chairs gave evidence — `1-4-throughout-my-report`: "the stature and profile of the PCC has, to some considerable extent, depended on the quality and personality of its Chairs. In the circumstances, I will refer to each of the four who gave evidence"
- Systems, not individuals — `1-4-throughout-my-report`: "Throughout, my Report has not sought to blame individuals but to focus on practices and systems. I will continue with this approach"
- His chapter on the commission — heading at the end of `j-proceedings-i-recommend-qualified`: "CHAPTER 4 THE PRESS COMPLAINTS COMMISSION AND ITS EFFECTIVENESS Introduction"; the card is para 1.1 of that chapter.
- (B only) Black — `4-1-pressbof-responsible-organisation`: "PressBoF, is responsible for the organisation and collection of the levy which funds the PCC"; "in turn appoint the Chair, currently Lord Black of Brentwood (who, between 1996 and 2003 was the Director of the PCC)". B not recommended: Black is also in the 4 Jan alternatives and "currently" is 2012.

Note: the 4 Nov hook is on the very next paragraph (1.2) and already said papers "could join or leave", which is close to this card's meaning. A avoids that angle, but this is the weakest of the eight; see final note.

### 2026-12-10 us-ica-2017-russia-elections:we-assess-high-confidence-gru:aed765f5

Card: "Disclosures through WikiLeaks did not contain any evident forgeries." (p. 3, the assessment's own judgment)

Candidates:
- A: Putin said publicly it was important the DNC data was exposed to WikiLeaks, while denying Russian "state-level" involvement. The 2017 US intelligence assessment says Russian military intelligence relayed it, then turns to the releases.
- B: The 2017 US intelligence assessment says Moscow most likely chose WikiLeaks for its self-proclaimed reputation for authenticity. It then turns to what WikiLeaks published.
- C: Russian military intelligence relayed material from the DNC and senior Democratic officials to WikiLeaks, the 2017 US intelligence assessment says with high confidence. It gives Moscow's likely reason, then turns to the releases.

Recommended: Putin said publicly it was important the DNC data was exposed to WikiLeaks, while denying Russian "state-level" involvement. The 2017 US intelligence assessment says Russian military intelligence relayed it, then turns to the releases.

Length: 235 (post 283)

Sources:
- Putin's statement and denial — `early-september-putin-said-publicly`: "In early September, Putin said publicly it was important the DNC data was exposed to WikiLeaks, calling the search for the source of the leaks a distraction and denying Russian “state-level” involvement." (No year in the sentence, so none in the hook.)
- GRU relayed it — `we-assess-high-confidence-gru`: "We assess with high confidence that the GRU relayed material it acquired from the DNC and senior Democratic officials to WikiLeaks."
- GRU = Russian military intelligence — `we-assess-high-confidence-russian`: "Russian military intelligence (General Staff Main Intelligence Directorate or GRU)"
- (B) `we-assess-high-confidence-gru`: "Moscow most likely chose WikiLeaks because of its self-proclaimed reputation for authenticity."
- "2017 US intelligence assessment": registry common name "2017 Intelligence Community Assessment".

Note: Putin's words are reported with his denial, so the hook does not put a one-sided quote in his mouth. "relayed it" keeps the report's verb and its "high confidence" framing is implicit in "says"; if the editor wants the confidence level explicit, C carries it.

### 2026-12-16 us-mueller-report:march-2016-gru-began-hacking:9a52bcde

- C: Stolen documents went out in 2016 under the names DCLeaks and Guccifer 2.0. The Mueller report says both were fictitious personas of the GRU, the Russian Army's intelligence service, and dates when its hacking began.

Recommended: The Mueller report says the GRU, the Russian Army's intelligence service, stole hundreds of thousands of documents in 2016. Its summary names the first targets.

Length: 160 (post 184)

Sources:

- Hundreds of thousands of documents — `march-2016-gru-began-hacking`: "The GRU stole hundreds of thousands of documents from the compromised email accounts and networks."
- 2016 — same paragraph dates the intrusions "In March 2016" and "In April 2016"; `same-time-ira-operation-began`: "in early 2016, the Russian government employed a second form of interference: cyber intrusions (hacking) and releases of hacked materials".
- "First targets" — the card is the first intrusion in the sequence; the next sentence is "In April 2016, the GRU hacked into the computer networks of the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) and the Democratic National Committee (DNC)."

### 2027-01-04 uk-leveson-inquiry:7-8-challenge-goes-too:1c7abcbf

Card: "I simply do not accept that these provisions will have a chilling effect on free speech or press freedom." (p. 1782, Lord Justice Leveson's own words)

Candidates:
- A: Lord Justice Leveson said that even if every major publisher joined an independent regulator the industry set up itself, he would still recommend a statute behind it. Some said that went too far.
- B: Lord Black, for the industry's Press Board of Finance, proposed a press regulator built on contracts between publishers. Lord Justice Leveson said that even if every major publisher signed up, he would still recommend a statute behind it. Some said that went too far.
- C: Lord Justice Leveson wanted a law to recognise an independent press regulator and support an arbitration system, even if every major publisher joined voluntarily. His report answers the charge that this went too far.

Recommended: Lord Justice Leveson said that even if every major publisher joined an independent regulator the industry set up itself, he would still recommend a statute behind it. Some said that went too far.

Length: 195 (post 222)

Sources:
- Statute even if all publishers join — `7-6-let-me-further`: "if an adequately independent regulatory body were to be established by industry and signed up to by all major news publishers, I still recommend the underpinning statute to provide for recognition of that body"
- "Some said that went too far" — `7-8-challenge-goes-too`: "As for the challenge that this goes too far, I simply do not accept..." (the card starts after this clause).
- (B) Lord Black — `1-12-i-can-deal`: "the proposals advanced by Lord Black of Brentwood (on behalf of the Press Board of Finance)"; `7-5-number-newspaper-groups`: "even if all the national newspaper publishers were to sign up to the contracts proposed by Lord Black". B is over 260 characters.
- (C) `7-6-let-me-further`: "support for an arbitration system". C's "recognise" echoes the 26 Oct and 6 Nov "recognised in law".

Note: the strategy calendar flags Leveson's regulation lines as the most contested sentences in the press debate; this card is one of them, in Leveson's own voice. The hook only sets up the dispute, without taking a side.

### 2027-01-15 uk-leveson-inquiry:3-23-another-revealing-answer:3df218e2

Candidates:
- A: Asked about his company's coverage of one story, Richard Desmond, proprietor of Express Newspapers, told the Leveson inquiry "we did do wrong" and apologised. Then he talked about regulation. The judge gave his view of that answer.
- B: Richard Desmond, proprietor of Express Newspapers, gave evidence to the Leveson inquiry on 12 January 2012. He apologised for his company's coverage of one story, then turned to regulation. Lord Justice Leveson wrote down how he read that answer.

Recommended: Asked about his company's coverage of one story, Richard Desmond, proprietor of Express Newspapers, told the Leveson inquiry "we did do wrong" and apologised. Then he talked about regulation. The judge gave his view of that answer.

Length: 231 (post 258)

Sources:
- Proprietor, asked about the topic, "we did do wrong" — `3-21-self-same-logic`: "the evidence of the proprietor of Express Newspapers, Richard Desmond, when he was asked about this topic. Mr Desmond said this: ''I'm not trying to win points here, because we did do wrong..."
- His company's conduct — `3-22-notwithstanding-language-deployed`: "this was an attempt by Mr Desmond to expiate, or at the very least to mitigate, his company's conduct"
- Apologised, then regulation — `3-22-notwithstanding-language-deployed`: "''and once again I do apologise to the McCanns, you know, et cetera, et cetera, et cetera ... What I think is free speech is very important and if we get any more regulation – I mean, what are we trying to do in this country?"
- (B) date — footnotes 35–38 cite "Transcript-of-Afternoon-Hearing-12-January-2012" for Desmond's evidence.

Note: the story is the Express titles' coverage of the McCann family; the hook does not name them (private individuals, a missing child). A gives Desmond's own acknowledgement and apology before the judge's view, so the post records both sides of the exchange; the card's "revealing"/"very disturbing" are Leveson's words and the hook marks them as the judge's.

### 2027-01-22 us-mueller-report:second-while-investigation-identified-numerous:976cdd52
